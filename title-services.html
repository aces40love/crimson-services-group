Title & settlement services

End-to-end coverage, from search to recording

A clear title is not a formality — it is what makes the sale hold up years later. Here is everything we handle.

Owner's title insurance

Protects the buyer against title defects, undisclosed liens, forged instruments, and heirs who surface after closing.

Lender's title insurance

Protects the lender's security interest for the life of the loan — required on virtually every financed transaction.

Title search & examination

A full public-records search for liens, easements, encumbrances, and breaks in the chain of title.

Escrow services

What to expect

The closing, step by step

Most people close on a house two or three times in their life. Here is the whole sequence, so nothing on closing day is a surprise.

Before you wire anything

Call our office at a number you already have and confirm the wire instructions verbally. We will never send changed wire instructions by email. Read the wire fraud guide →

1

Contract & order opened

We receive the executed contract, open the file, and confirm the closing date with all parties.

2

Title search & examination

Public records searched for liens, judgments, easements, and chain-of-title defects.

3

Commitment issued & cleared

Requirements and exceptions are listed, then cleared — payoffs ordered, releases obtained.

4

Closing disclosure & figures

Final numbers reconciled with the lender and reviewed with buyer and seller in advance.

5

Signing

Typically one to two hours. Bring a government photo ID and, for cash to close, a wire sent after verbal verification.

6

Funding, recording & policy

Funds disburse, the deed and deed of trust record with the county, and the final policy issues.

Title FAQ

Common title & closing questions

Custom in Tennessee generally has the seller paying for the owner's policy that protects the buyer, and the buyer paying for the lender's policy. Custom is not law — allocation is negotiable and is set by your purchase contract.
The lender's policy protects the lender, not you. An owner's policy is what defends your equity against a forged deed, an unknown heir, an unreleased lien, or a boundary claim — and it is a one-time premium that lasts as long as you own the property.
Typically real estate commissions, the owner's title policy, state transfer tax of $0.37 per $100 of consideration, prorated property taxes, recording fees, and any negotiated concessions — commonly landing in the 6–10% range with commissions as the largest piece. Use the net sheet above for your specific numbers.
Thirty to forty-five days for a financed purchase; ten to fifteen days for cash. The pace is usually set by the lender's underwriting, the property valuation, and how quickly title requirements clear.
A valid government-issued photo ID, your Social Security number, proof of homeowners insurance for a purchase, and any documents your lender requested. Cash to close should be wired in advance — after you verbally verify the instructions — or brought as a cashier's check if the amount is small.
